  `temp/advanced_lines_plan.md`: `Lines.generate` now produces a continuous
  zero-gap annular border from a smooth closed cubic path, with traversal-aware
  cap-contact checks at every join including the closing seam, and a new
  `src/levels/squiggly_circle.lua` level following

## Changes Made
- `src/systems/lines.lua`: removed the closed-path rejection in
  `emit_biarc_annular`; the shared `Path:validate_joins()` call still gates
  fitting and now reports the failing segment pair plus a closing-seam marker.
  Added `CONTACT_TOLERANCE = 1e-4` and `validate_contacts`, which requires
  zero-gap annular neighbors to share a full cap (both endpoint pairings are
  tried so inflection joins with flipped radial orientation pass without false
  full-thickness gaps). `Lines.generate` checks every emitted join and
  `Lines.validate` checks every zero-gap annular line group, including the
  seam. `validate_overlaps` gained a conservative bounding-disk broadphase
  built from the emitted polygon centroid/radius.
- `src/entities/peg.lua`: annular polygon resolution is now span-aware. A full
  turn keeps the historical `max(16, floor(r_out * 0.4))` count; a partial
  sector scales the count with its angular span (minimum 4). Narrow generated
  pieces keep ~15.7 px chords instead of ~580 near-duplicate vertices. Annular
  collision is analytic, so physics is unaffected; full-circle output is
  unchanged.
- `src/systems/level_validator.lua`: annular pegs are bounded by their emitted
  polygon AABB instead of `r_out` around the arc center. Large-radius biarcs
  near inflections have far-away arc centers; the old test discarded valid
  pieces and broke line contiguity at load.
- `src/levels/squiggly_circle.lua`: one 192-piece zero-gap annular border from
  a 96-segment cubic Hermite path with analytic polar tangents and a seam that
  reuses the first sample; thickness 20, max piece length 40,
  `curve_tolerance = 0.25` (authored cubics, not the ideal polar curve); seed
  20260918, red 10 / green 2, 1200x1900.
- `tests/test_lines.lua`: +11 tests. Exact 96-segment squiggle fixture, an
  8-segment convex closed cubic loop, an exact reversed traversal, a polygon
  coverage metric, and cases for cap sharing, seam-corner context, inflected

- `tests/test_squiggly_level.lua` (new): +7 tests. Level schema and static

## Decisions & Learnings
- Inflection gate: the exact target fixture fits with the existing recursive
  biarc fitter in both traversal directions (192 pieces, worst cap mismatch
  ~2e-12 px). Isolated inflected and near-straight cubics pass as well. A
  deliberately extreme S cubic fails cleanly after bounded subdivision because
  its minimum curvature radius (9.2588 px) is below the 10 px half thickness —
  a positive inner radius is impossible, not a fitter defect. The fixture is
  recorded in `temp/squiggly_strong_s_fixture.lua`.
- Cap contact is separate from overlap. A shared cap is verified by matching
  both endpoints of one emitted cap to a cap of the neighbor, trying both
  radial pairings; traversal direction is implicit, so sweep sign and
  curvature sign changes cannot create reversed-cap false gaps.
- `CONTACT_TOLERANCE` is independent of `curve_tolerance`: it only measures
  emitted boundary agreement. At 1e-4 px it absorbs ~1e-12 px trig rounding
  and the 1e-7 px slack `Path.new` allows between source segments, while
  staying 100x below the 0.01 px `annular_cap_epsilon` used by collision.
- The old annular polygon count was per-piece, not span-aware: a 3-degree
  sector and a full circle both tessellated at `floor(r_out * 0.4)` segments.
  Scaling the count with span fixes the quadratic SAT cost without changing
  the physical chord resolution. The squiggle overlap pass went from 4.8 s to
  ~1 ms, and `check_level` from unusable to 0.13 s.
- The boundary filter must test the emitted shape, not the analytic one:
  `r_out` is a correct reach from the arc center for broadphase and rotation
  sweeping, but wrong for deciding whether a sector intersects a wall.
- Real physics crosses the generated seams cleanly: grazes transition from
  one piece to its neighbor in both traversal directions, both neighbors score
  as intentional shared-boundary contacts, and high-speed direct approaches
  from both sides never tunnel. No cap-collision artifact was reproduced.
